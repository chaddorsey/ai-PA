Steve Ruiz makes a compelling case for building more cheap microcontroller development boards, particularly ESP32-S3 boards that cost around $25. He argues these boards are fundamentally different from Raspberry Pis—they're complete devices (not computers) packed with displays, batteries, wireless connectivity, speakers, and sensors.

The key insight is that coding these boards has become dramatically easier with AI assistance. Using Claude Code, developers can go from design to working hardware in an hour by having Claude work through datasheets and documentation. Ruiz demonstrates this "vibe coding" approach works reliably for small hardware projects.

Since his initial post about ESP32 development, he's seen an explosion of friends and colleagues building creative applications with these boards—everything from keyboarded devices to AMOLED displays to devices with dials and headphone jacks. The low cost ($25 or less) makes them accessible for experimentation and learning.

## Key Takeaways

- ESP32-S3 boards are mass-produced devices costing just a few dollars wholesale, enabling affordable consumer development boards
- Modern AI assistants (Claude) can reliably program these boards even without deep embedded systems expertise
- "Vibe coding" with AI for hardware is practical and productive—spending token budget to save development time is a good tradeoff
- Cheap hardware enables experimentation: mid-career programmers are building novel applications because the barrier to entry is so low
- Market opportunity: manufacturers should build more form factors and device types at this price point
